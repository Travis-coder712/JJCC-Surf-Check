# JJCC Surf Check — data & method review

Friday 30th October 2026 · reviewed against v1.0.0 (`app.js`, last commit `daf019e`)

Everything below was checked against live data rather than read off the code: the app's own scoring
functions were run on today's Open-Meteo feed, its tide model was compared with BOM's official
predictions, and every beach's coordinates and facing were checked against the OpenStreetMap coastline.

---

## Verdict

The app's structure is reasonable. It uses free model data, gives each beach a profile, and scores
each hour. But four foundations are wrong, and they cause the problems you've noticed:

1. **The tides are made up.** The "5-constituent harmonic model calibrated to Torquay" is not
   calibrated to anything. It is up to **2 h 40 min out** and gets the big-low/small-low pattern backwards.
2. **Several beaches face the wrong way.** That includes Jan Juc, which is about 60° off, so the
   offshore/onshore logic and the map arrows are wrong at your home break.
3. **The score is an average, so good swell hides bad wind.** A 15 km/h onshore at Jan Juc still
   comes out "Good/Great". That's why you see "great for 8 hours".
4. **The data is coarser than it looks.** All 10 beaches share **one** wind reading, taken from a
   land cell behind Anglesea. Torquay, Jan Juc, 13th Beach and Bells all share **one** wave grid cell.

The fix is a rebuild of the data layer and the scoring engine, not more tuning. The Beyond-the-Surf
content (eats, movies, snorkelling) can stay as it is.

## 1. Tides — confirmed inaccurate

BOM's nearest official open-coast port is **Point Lonsdale** (`VIC_TP009`, about 30 km east of Torquay).
Here it is against the app and against Open-Meteo's modelled sea level, for today:

| Event | BOM Point Lonsdale (official) | App model | Open-Meteo sea level |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | 3:42 am · 1.80 m | 1:00 am (**−2 h 42**) | 2:45 am (−57 min) |
| Low | 9:13 am · 0.65 m | 8:00 am (−1 h 13) | 8:30 am (−43 min) |
| High | 3:22 pm · 1.52 m | 2:30 pm (−52 min) | 2:30 pm (−52 min) |
| Low | 9:19 pm · **0.13 m (big low)** | 8:00 pm · 0.54 m (**small low**) | 8:30 pm (big low ✓) |

There are three problems with the model:
- The amplitudes and phases are invented, so the error drifts from day to day. Some days look fine and others are hours out.
- The diurnal inequality is backwards. The app thinks the evening low is the shallow one, when it is actually the deepest of the day.
- "Low / Mid / High" uses fixed height cut-offs (< 0.6 m, > 1.0 m). Our tides have a strong
  day-to-day inequality, so a fixed cut-off mislabels whole tides. One day's "high" is another day's "mid".

**Fix:** use BOM Point Lonsdale predictions as ground truth. Two ways to do that:
- (a) A monthly GitHub Action fetches the next 12 months of highs and lows into `tides.json`.
- (b) Fit a proper 8–10-constituent harmonic model to a year of BOM data, so it runs offline and doesn't expire.

Then add a small Torquay time offset and interpolate between highs and lows with a cosine curve.
Express tide state relative to **that tide's own range**: "2 h after low, 40 % of the way up,
rising" rather than "Mid".

## 2. Beach geometry — several beaches face the wrong way

The shore-normal ("which way you look out to sea") was computed from the OSM coastline:

| Beach | App `facing` | Actual (OSM) | App coordinate | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jan Juc** | 200° SSW | **~135–143° SE** | OK | Offshore should be ~NW, not NNE (20°). The app scores **NE winds as offshore** at Jan Juc, when they're cross-shore. |
| Bells | 168° | ~135–138° SE | OK | Offshore ~NW (315°), app has 330°. Minor. |
| Torquay Surf Beach | 155° | ~158–170° | **520 m inland** | Facing is about right; the marker is off. |
| Torquay Front | 90° E | ~113° ESE | OK | Minor. |
| Point Addis | 155° | ~122–136° | **510 m inland** | Moderate. |
| Anglesea | 155° | ~139–149° | 330 m off | Minor. |
| **Point Roadknight** | 45° NE | ~120–135° ESE | **720 m off, wrong spot**: it's on the Anglesea river/town, ~2.5 km from the point | Map, arrows and facing all wrong. Its real feature is *shelter from SW* by the point, which a single "facing" angle can't represent. |
| Lorne | 115° | ~70–90° E | OK | Moderate. |
| Fairhaven | 180° | ~172–179° | **700 m inland** | Facing correct. |
| 13th Beach | 180° | ~187–191° at the SLSC | **940 m inland** | Facing about right; the marker is off. |

**Why the map arrows look wrong.** The arrows aren't placed on the beach at all. They sit in a fixed
corner legend box, rotated from the `facing` and `idealOffshore` values above, so wherever those
values are wrong the arrows are wrong too. The maps are also centred on separate `mapLat/mapLng`
points, some of which aren't on the beach.

**Fix:** move every coordinate onto the actual break. Replace the single `idealOffshore` angle with a
**curated wind-sector table per spot** (offshore / cross-off / cross / onshore / *sheltered*). Draw
those sectors as wedges over the water on the satellite map, with the current wind arrow pointing
at the beach itself.

## 3. Scoring — why "great for 8 hours"

The rating is a weighted **average**:

> period 25 % + height 20 % + wind dir 20 % + wind speed 15 % + swell dir 10 % + tide 10 %

Swell-related terms make up 55 % of that, and they barely change from hour to hour. Wind is only
35 %, so a day with decent swell keeps scoring 60–75 whatever the wind does. Running the real code
on today's data:

- Jan Juc, tomorrow 12 pm: **WNW 34 km/h** (blown out) still scores **59 "Good"**.
- Jan Juc and 13th Beach produce **identical scores hour for hour**, because they have the same wave cell, the same wind point and the same `idealOffshore`.
- **Family mode ranks Jan Juc #1 at 6 am (83)**, above Roadknight. The family-safety rating only
  nudges the score by ±6, so a hazardous reef beats the safe beach.
- The "good window" only ends when the score falls 15 points *and* crosses a band edge. With a
  flat, averaged score that rarely happens, so the window runs for the whole look-ahead.
- Tide is a cliff edge (100 → 20), so the score jumps at an arbitrary height threshold.

Surf quality isn't an average. It's a set of **limiting factors**: no swell means no surf, however
nice the wind, and strong onshore wind wrecks good swell.

**Fix:** score each factor separately, then combine them so the weakest one caps the result (multiply them or take the minimum):

```
quality = sizeFit(spot, surfHeight) × windFactor(spot sector, speed, gusts) × tideFit(spot, tide stage, size) × swellDirFit
```

Map the result onto a scale surfers already use, **Flat · Poor · Poor-Fair · Fair · Good · Very Good · Epic**,
and pair it with the subtext you suggested, written from whichever factor is limiting:

> **Fair+ now · Jan Juc** — "Best on low-mid; it's mid and filling in now. Peaks ~6:40 am at low.
> NNW 12 km/h, offshore until the SE sea breeze ~11 am."

Windows become **runs of consecutive 15-minute slots where the rating stays at or above Fair**. The
"why it ends" is whichever factor drops first: wind, tide or swell. Show them as a timeline bar,
not a single "for N hours" number.

## 4. Data sources — what's being used and what's better

## 5. Other bugs found

- **"Best today" goes blank before 11 am.** It uses `new Date().toISOString().slice(0,10)`, which is the
  **UTC** date. Before 11 am AEDT that's yesterday, so today's list filters to nothing and today is
  treated as a "future day".
- Default mode is Family.
- The scoring guide claims the tide model is "calibrated to Torquay tidal data". It isn't, and that text needs to go.

## 6. Recommended way forward

**Phase 1: get the data and engine right (the important bit)**
1. Spot profiles rebuilt from geometry plus local knowledge. Each spot gets: coordinates on the break,
   a wind-sector table, a swell-direction transfer, a size range it works in, a tide preference curve
   (which can depend on size, e.g. "low only works when it's small"), skill level and hazards.
   *Some of this is your call. I've confirmed the geometry, but you know which tide Jan Juc's
   bowl really wants.*
2. New tides from BOM (§1), new waves with partitions and breaking height (§4), wind per area plus live obs.
3. New rating engine with limiting factors, a 7-step scale, subtext, and 15-minute windows (§3).
4. A **backtest page** for the scoring engine: today's forecast against BOM obs and BOM tides, so accuracy can be checked rather than assumed.

**Phase 2: make it the Surf Coast bible**
- Home screen: "Where should we go right now", with the top 3 spots, why each one, and when it changes.
- Per-spot page: a timeline (size, wind, tide, rating over the day), how the spot works, what makes it fire, hazards, access.
- Add the missing core breaks: **Winkipop, Rincon, Torquay Point, Point Danger, Southside**.
- A **Surf diary**: rate the session you actually had. Over time that becomes the calibration data.

**Phase 3: look and feel**
- A calm, editorial "surf report" look: dark navy and sand, one accent colour, real typography,
  data-led charts. No emoji everywhere, no celebration overlays, no sounds.
- Keep Family / Surfer / Logger as one quiet setting, not a big toggle.

**Hosting:** stays a static PWA on GitHub Pages, plus one small Cloudflare Worker for BOM data (wind obs, and tides if we go
the fetch route). You'd need to deploy the Worker; everything else ships the way it does now.
